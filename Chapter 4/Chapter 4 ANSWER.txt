Exercise 2
4.5 Four Basic Elements of Counter-Controlled Repetition
Initialization: Initializes the counter variable.
Condition: Tests whether the loop should continue.
Increment or decrement: Updates the counter.
Termination: Ends the loop when the condition becomes false.
4.6 Compare and Contrast while and for

Similarities:

Both are repetition statements.
Both execute statements repeatedly while a condition is true.
Both can be used for counter-controlled repetition.

Differences:

The while loop places initialization, condition, and counter updates separately.
The for loop places initialization, condition, and update in its header.
The while loop is suitable when the number of repetitions is not known in advance; the for loop is convenient when it is known.
4.7 Situation Where do...while Is More Appropriate

A do...while loop is appropriate for a menu-driven program that must display a menu at least once before checking the user's choice.

4.8 Compare and Contrast break and continue
break: Immediately terminates the loop or switch statement.
continue: Skips the remaining statements in the current loop iteration and proceeds to the next iteration.
